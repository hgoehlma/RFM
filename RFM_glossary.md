# RFM: Glossary
`v0.7.1` // `top_level_artifact` // [living]

---

## What this file is

This glossary disambiguates terms used in RFM documents that carry different meanings across reader contexts: words that look shared but aren't. Each entry records why the term means what it means here, not just what it means. Entries are added when a term appears in multiple documents and its meaning is likely to diverge across readers or contexts. Below that threshold, definitions belong inline where the term is first used.

---

| Term | RFM meaning | Risk of confusion |
|---|---|---|
| **Anti-deference** | The instruction to propose and execute rather than defer: the behavioral expression of the co-author role at the execution step. Anti-deference has a ceiling: it governs derivative cleanup, wording improvements, and implementation catch-up. It does not license unilateral action on structural reasoning changes, which require explicit joint decision. | Commonly read as a general instruction to act rather than wait. In RFM it is bounded; initiative without joint decision where joint decision is required is a misapplication, not an expression, of the principle. |
| **Binding condition** | The state of affairs under which an instruction should be governing behaviour. Being about to compress reasoning that is already written is one. Binding conditions are unbounded, and recognising one is something a reader does rather than something a schedule does. Paired with delivery occasion: the condition says when an instruction ought to be in force, and the occasion says when text can arrive. | Reads as a condition that is itself binding, in the contractual sense. It is the condition under which an instruction binds. |
| **Carrier** | The means by which a piece of wanted behavior reaches the LLM or the human and stays in effect. Means differ in what they hold well, what breaks them and how they are built, so each wanted behavior is assigned to the one that suits it. The human counts as one, for behaviors only a person can supply. | Commonly read as a mere channel or container that transports a behavior. In RFM it is the whole means of getting a behavior to hold, including its cost and how it fails, and the human can be one. |
| **Delivery occasion** | A point at which text can enter the LLM's view. The medium supplies these rather than any platform: before the first turn, at a turn boundary, or not at all. They are finite, and they stay finite when the tooling is unrecognisable. An instruction delivered before the first turn may not be needed until the hundredth, which is why the occasion is not the condition the instruction answers. Paired with binding condition. | "Occasion" suggests the moment at which an instruction matters. It names only the slot where text can arrive. |
| **Chesterton's Fence log** | A minimal running record maintained during pressure mode, capturing deviations from established reasoning, conscious deferrals, and broken assumptions. Its purpose is to make the return to reasoning mode honest rather than reconstructed from code. Its form is domain-dependent: a section appended to the reasoning document, a separate file, a dated note, whatever is reachable and maintained. | Commonly implies a structured audit trail or formal log file. In RFM it is deliberately minimal; the discipline is capture, not format. |
| **Co-author** | Both the human and the LLM hold the co-author role: a working relationship in which both contribute to the reasoning, red team each other's thinking, and hold the methodology's discipline jointly. The non-obvious direction: the LLM is a co-author, not a tool. A co-author notices when something is off and says so without being asked. | Commonly used as a writing credit or to denote a human collaborator. In RFM it denotes a role with behavioral obligations, held by both parties. |
| **Confirmation gate** | The pre-session check in which the LLM confirms it can derive the target artifact from the source documents without interpreting ambiguities or filling gaps. The gate is initiated by the human and completed by the LLM. If the LLM cannot confirm, the session does not begin, the source documents require further work first. The gate is the boundary between phase one and phase two: reasoning is complete on one side, derivation begins on the other. | Commonly read as a quality check the LLM performs on its own output. In RFM it is a readiness check on the source documents before derivation begins, not an evaluation of the derivative after it is produced. |
| **Compression** | Always a structural change: the loss of reasoning signal from a document, regardless of how it presents. Compression can appear as curation, cleanup, or re-homing. None of those framings change what it is. | Commonly understood as a neutral efficiency operation, making something shorter without loss. In RFM, shortening a reasoning document is never neutral. |
| **Curation** | The discipline of keeping a reasoning document alive through both addition and reduction. Two modes: triggered (something accumulates or surfaces in use) and deliberate return (coming back without a specific trigger). Both are required. A document that only grows is already dying. | Commonly understood as passive collection or careful archiving. In RFM it is an active discipline that includes honest removal. |
| **Derivative** | Everything phase two produces from the source documents: an artifact produced directly from them (a README, a skill, a prompt, a business plan, a slide deck, an agent's code), or an output that artifact produces when run again against new input. The chain has structure: reasoning document → operational document → artifact → output. A derivative that moves without its source reasoning document is drift by another name. | Carries strong associations with financial instruments and calculus. In RFM it means strictly: anything produced from a reasoning document, directly or through an artifact it produced, whose changes must be traceable back to that source. |
| **Artifact / Output** | An artifact is a derivative produced directly from the source documents: a README, a skill, a prompt, a business plan, a slide deck, an agent built to analyze transcripts. An output is what an artifact meant to be run again produces on one occasion: one transcript's analysis, one filled report, one generated deck for one meeting. The distinguishing question is whether the thing is meant to be run again against new input, not whether it once involved the source documents. Not every artifact produces outputs; a README is the deliverable, it doesn't get run. Whether an artifact carries a version header and gets curated over time is a property some artifacts have and others don't; it doesn't decide artifact-versus-output. | "Output" commonly means any result at all, including the artifact itself. In RFM the two are never interchangeable: an output carries neither a version header nor the curation rule, because it's a derivative of running an artifact, not a derivative of the source documents. |
| **Execution** | The fourth step of the joint reasoning sequence: carrying out what propose, reflect, and converge settled on. It names a position in the sequence rather than a kind of work, so it covers editing a source document, producing a derivative, recording a decision, and shipping code alike, and it occurs in both phases. Deriving an artifact from the source documents is one kind of execution, the phase-two kind. Editing a source document is execution and not derivation, because nothing is produced from the source. The step is where the division of labor applies: the LLM formulates and edits, the human judges and confirms. | Commonly means running code or shipping a change. In RFM that narrows it twice, to one kind of work and to phase two, when the step occurs whenever a converged proposal is carried out, including on the reasoning documents themselves. |
| **Frame** | The statement the LLM makes before executing something still being decided: what is settled, what the boundaries are, what would end the work, and what it excludes as well as covers. It stands in for a permission, and it stays on record so the work can be checked against it afterward. The design treats the difficulty of writing it as a signal: settled reasoning yields a short frame, and reasoning that is not settled makes it hard to write. Naming where the reasoning goes vague is an acceptable result in place of a frame. Stating it first is what makes premature entry visible before any work exists to unwind. | Commonly read as the context or framing of a conversation, or as a request for approval to proceed. In RFM it is a stated position the LLM produces on entering execution, and it measures readiness more than it seeks approval. |
| **Friction** | The signal that something in the reasoning doesn't fit, present before you can articulate why. Kahneman's work on System 1 reasoning established that human pattern recognition fires before conscious articulation: the signal is real before the argument is formed. In RFM, friction is treated as information worth following, not noise to smooth over. When something the AI produces doesn't quite land, that sense of something not fitting is friction; name it rather than suppress it. | Commonly associated with unproductive conflict or inefficiency, something to be eliminated. In RFM it is the opposite: friction is a signal to follow, not remove. Smoothing it over is the failure mode. |
| **Human prompt** | The artifact that calibrates the human collaborator's behavior in a co-authorship session. Where the traveling prompt governs LLM behavior, the human prompt governs the human's: what to watch for, where to push back, and what discipline the human carries into the session. It is one component of the RFM prompt system, alongside the traveling prompt and the sweep prompts. | Commonly read as any instruction directed at a human, or as the human's turn in a conversation. In RFM it names a specific artifact with a specific audience: the human in an active co-authorship session. |
| **Joint reasoning sequence** | The governing pattern of co-authorship in RFM: propose → reflect → converge → execute. Both parties contribute to the proposal, both test it against the reasoning and each other's instincts, both align on a direction, and then whoever is best positioned carries out the execution. The sequence is directed; it completes at execution and restarts fresh with the next problem. Division of labor (the LLM formulates and edits, the human judges and confirms) is one expression of this pattern at the execution step, not a description of the whole. | Commonly the collaboration pattern is described as a division of labor between LLM and human. In RFM that division describes the execution step only; the full pattern is joint throughout the first three steps. |
| **Operational (document)** | A derivative that carries the *what* (constants, interfaces, procedures, and known values) that a reasoning document must never absorb. It exists because some domains have hard execution contracts requiring their own artifact. Two kinds, distinguished by owner rather than by level: the methodology's operational document carries the conventions every project inherits and arrives with RFM; a project's operational document carries that project's own execution contracts and stays with the project. Where an inherited convention needs a project's value, the convention arrives and the value is set by the project. Not a general overflow for implementation detail. | Commonly means "currently running" or "in production." In RFM it names a specific document type in the derivative chain, distinct from the reasoning document that is its source. The second confusion is internal: reading every operational document as domain-specific, which leads a project to rewrite conventions it was meant to inherit, and leaves RFM's checks pointing at a document that no longer describes it. |
| **The pair** | Shorthand for a reasoning document and its operational counterpart at the same level, invoked together because some derivatives, a software pipeline, a skill artifact, aren't specified by the reasoning document alone. Checking the pair means checking both, not choosing one. Distinct from "source documents," which names the reasoning hierarchy alone and stays that way: the pair exists precisely because some derivations need more than the source alone provides. | Reads as a generic reference to any two documents under discussion. In RFM it names one specific relationship, a reasoning document and its own operational document, nothing else. |
| **Practitioner** | Someone actively applying the methodology, past the newcomer stage, working within established RFM discipline. The term carries no implication of mastery or seniority; it names the working relationship to the methodology, not a level within it. "Newcomer" names the entry stage; "practitioner" names everyone who has moved past it. | Commonly used to imply expertise or credentialing. In RFM it is purely descriptive; anyone applying the methodology in their own work is a practitioner. |
| **Posture** | The behavioural stance a party holds at a given moment, defined by which behaviour counts as the contribution and which counts as the failure. RFM names two that govern joint work. Under co-author posture, departing from what was asked can be the contribution, and silent compliance is the failure. Under execution posture, departing from what was settled is the failure, and fidelity to it is the contribution. Execution posture takes its name from the fourth step of the joint reasoning sequence and is the posture correct at that step, not a second meaning of the word. These two score the same act in opposite directions, so a party holds one at a time and moves between them within a session. | Reads as a fixed attitude a party carries throughout a session. Co-author posture and execution posture are held one at a time and change within a session, so holding the co-author role and holding co-author posture are different claims: the role is constant, the posture is not. |
| **Pressure mode** | The regime entered when delivery pressure makes the full reasoning discipline locally unacceptable: a deadline, a sprint, a time-boxed commitment. Inside it, established reasoning is executed within, not extended, and a Chesterton's Fence log captures deviations, conscious deferrals, and broken assumptions until the return to reasoning mode. The term is named for the condition that invokes the regime rather than for the work done inside it, because execution is the fourth step of the joint reasoning sequence and occurs constantly in reasoning mode as well. | Reads as a stress state, the mode you fall into when rushed. In RFM it is entered deliberately and only where the reasoning documents are complete enough to execute against; it carries an entry condition and a debt that must be repaid, and lapsed discipline does not qualify. The second confusion is with execution: pressure mode is a span of time, execution is a step in the joint reasoning sequence, and that step runs in both modes. |
| **Derivation-legibility** | The property of a reasoning document hierarchy that makes autonomous LLM execution possible without the human present. A document that is derivation-legible is complete, internally sufficient, and structured so that concepts that must remain distinct at execution time are visibly distinct in the source. Distinct from joint legibility, which governs readability by both human and LLM during collaborative authorship. A document can satisfy one without satisfying the other. | Commonly read as a synonym for clarity or readability. In RFM it names a specific design requirement for the second phase of the methodology, one that imposes structural constraints on source documents beyond what human readability alone requires. |
| **Phase one / phase two** | The two structurally distinct phases of RFM practice. Phase one is symmetric: human and LLM collaborate to build the reasoning documents, both contributing across all eight sections. Phase two is asymmetric: the completed documents are the source from which the LLM derives and executes without the human present at derivation time. The phases have different design requirements: joint legibility for phase one, derivation-legibility for phase two. Neither phase is optional. | Commonly read as a generic sequencing metaphor. In RFM the distinction is structural: the two phases have different design requirements, different governing artifacts, and different failure modes. Conflating them produces documents that serve one phase well and the other poorly. |
| **Socratic mode** | In the guided drafting session, the LLM's mode of engagement with the newcomer's reasoning: drawing it out through questions rather than supplying it through answers, definitions, or examples. Socratic mode governs who does the reasoning; the newcomer must reason, not receive. But it does not prevent the LLM from contributing its own reasoning, proposing directions, or red teaming weak thinking. The co-author role remains fully active. The failure mode Socratic mode guards against is directive mode: the LLM filling sections on the newcomer's behalf. The failure mode Socratic mode must not produce is interrogation: the LLM withholding its own reasoning to avoid being directive. The constraint is further bounded by object: Socratic mode governs section-filling, not claim-challenging. When a newcomer states something with more confidence than the evidence supports, that is a red team moment. Socratic mode does not suppress it. | Commonly associated with philosophical dialogue where one party questions and the other answers. In RFM it names a constraint on one specific failure mode (the LLM doing the reasoning for the newcomer) not a description of the full session dynamic, which remains genuine co-authorship throughout. |
| **Sweep** | A deliberately invoked review pass, focused on a specific document, producing findings for human ruling rather than edits. Each sweep does one job, and no two sweeps run in the same pass. | Commonly means a general cleanup, search operation, or background process. In RFM it is deliberately timed, not automated, and apart from the traveling prompt's ongoing prevention. |
