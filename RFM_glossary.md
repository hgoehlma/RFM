# RFM: Glossary

---

## What this file is

This glossary holds the terms several RFM documents use and a reader could take in another sense. A term used in only one document, or whose everyday sense is the right one, is explained where it is used. Each entry says what the term means here and why, and how it tends to be misread.

---

| Term | RFM meaning | Risk of confusion |
|---|---|---|
| **Break condition** | The condition under which an assumption stops holding. Every assumption in a reasoning document states one, because a later reader has to be able to tell when the direction needs reopening. | Reads as the test that ends a loop in a program. |
| **Carrier** | What puts a behavior in front of whoever is acting, at the moment it is needed: the always-on instructions, a skill, a hook, a subagent handed a task, the human guide. Behaviors RFM needs do not happen on their own: the LLM's training pulls against several of them, and the human is pulled against others. | Reads as a container that moves text around. A carrier is chosen by whether the behavior happens at that moment, so what it costs and how it fails are part of the choice. |
| **Curation** | Keeping a document useful to a later reader: adding what has become true and removing what no longer does a job. Age does not decide this. A rejected option stays while someone might try it again, and a Hard Lesson stays while its failure can still happen. | Reads as careful collecting or archiving, or as compression. Compression says the same thing in fewer words, and an LLM doing it can lose the reasons along the way. Curation removes what no longer does a job and keeps the reasons for what stays. |
| **Derivative** | What is produced from the sources: code, a template, a prompt, a skill, a plan, a deck. The sources are the reasoning and operational documents it comes from, and the glossary. A change starts in the sources and the derivative follows, so the reasons behind it stay in one place. What a derivative produces when it runs, such as the CSV files a script writes, is its output. An output is not curated: if one is wrong, the fix goes into what produced it. | Reads as anything loosely based on something else, or as a term from finance or calculus. "Source" can also be read as a cited reference. |
| **Graduating** | Moving an entry to the place where it now does its job, with its reason, and removing it from where it was. A Hard Lesson fixed by a change becomes the reason for that change in the Chosen Direction. An Open Question becomes an assumption, with its break condition, once we settle on a belief. An entry with no job left is cut instead. | Reads as an entry being finished or promoted. Nothing ranks higher for having moved. |
| **Module** | A part of the work with its own reasoning document, and often an operational document, under a parent in the hierarchy. Documents grow and shrink as the work moves. When a reasoning document grows a lot, we call that strain, a sign to ask whether it holds several components and whether one should branch into its own module. No size decides this. | Reads as a component of a program. Code can be derived from a module, but the module is the reasoning about that part of the work. |
| **Operational** | A document that builds on its reasoning document and gives the what: the values, interfaces and procedures that deriving needs and the reasoning does not specify. The human and the LLM write it while they reason, and a part of the work gets one only where it needs one. RFM's top-level operational document holds the conventions every project uses. | Reads as currently running or in production. It is also not a derivative of its reasoning document, because the human and the LLM write it as part of the sources. |
| **Partner** | The human and the LLM as two parties who stand equal, each bringing what the other lacks, and either able to propose, push back or say no. "Colleague" and "teammate" are used for work handed off and managed, "collaborator" does not say the two stand equal, and "co-author" narrows the work to writing. | Reads as a business relationship, or as a claim about what an LLM is. The word describes how the two work together. |
| **Practitioner** | Anyone using RFM in their work, however new to it. The word names the person in relation to RFM or a project: someone who takes it up, sets up a project with it, or forks it. Inside a session, next to the LLM, the same person is "the human". | Reads as an expert or someone with credentials. What RFM asks of a practitioner is the right mindset: wanting a partner and doing their own part of the thinking, not knowing how many sections a document has. |
| **Sweep** | A review of a document that the human or the LLM proposes, doing one kind of reading: structure, language, or across documents. It is run on a new document and again whenever other work may have left passages stale or content in the wrong place. It runs in a fresh context, because someone who wrote a document reads its faults as sound. A sweep returns findings and changes nothing. The human and the LLM then work out together what to do with each one. | Reads as a general cleanup, or as a single check that is done once. It also does not run automatically in the background. |
