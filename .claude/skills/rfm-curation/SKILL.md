# Curation

A document holds only what is true now. Earlier versions are in the project's history. An entry stays while it does its section's job for a reader now, and goes when that job cannot be named. Ask what job an entry does, not whether there is a reason to remove it. Whoever proposes a cut, either of us, brings a view on it, and we agree before the text changes.

## Each section's job

**The Problem.** The gap that justifies the work, named as a gap, not as a project, with no solution language. It changes when the gap changes.

**The Assumptions.** A belief the direction depends on, kept only if its being false would change the direction. Each states the condition under which it breaks. Not implementation detail. An assumption stays while the direction depends on it and it has not broken. When it breaks, the direction that rested on it is reopened.

**The Landscape.** What already exists and why each approach falls short for this problem. It distinguishes and does not choose. A row stays while someone hearing the problem would still reach for it, so the Landscape is seldom cut.

**The Options Considered.** Solutions to this Problem that were tried, or worked through seriously enough that the reasons they lost took real work to find, each with the failure that ruled it out. Not design choices made in passing, not alternatives dismissed in a sentence. Empty when there were none. An option stays while someone might try it again and the reason it lost still holds.

**The Chosen Direction and Why.** Why this direction over the others. No how, no restated option. When the direction is replaced, the old one moves to the Options Considered if someone might reach for it again. Otherwise it goes.

**The Boundaries.** What the work does not cover. Not a constraint on decisions, not space nobody has designed yet. A boundary stays while it still marks what the work does not cover.

**The Open Questions.** Unknowns whose answer would change the reasoning. A deferred decision belongs in the plan. A question stays while it is still unknown. One whose answer would change nothing goes.

**The Hard Lessons.** A failure that happened, can still happen, and that nothing has fixed: what fails and why, not when it happened. A failure only foreseen is an assumption or an open question instead. Where a change can fix the failure now, we make the change and write no lesson. A lesson stays while its failure can still happen.

Apply the test to what an entry contains, not to its heading. An entry that repeats another does no job of its own.

## Resolving

- A hard lesson resolved by a change: its failure becomes the reason for that change in the Chosen Direction, and the lesson leaves.
- An answered Open Question goes where its answer does a job. A settled belief becomes an assumption with its break condition. A commitment goes to the Chosen Direction, a limit to the Boundaries, something that holds only in one module to that module's document.
- Carry the reason with the resolution. Remove the entry from its old place in the same change. State the passages that hedged on the question as firmly as the answer holds.

## Two brakes

- Merging: when a cut merges entries or sentences, name what was distinct about the things merged and say whether that distinction still matters. Agree on it before cutting.
- A hard lesson: name the change that fixed its failure, or why the failure can no longer happen. If neither can be named, it stays.

Everywhere else the job test decides.

## Branching

- Remove from the parent what the module now carries, in the same change.
- Look below the moved reasoning in the tree. What derives from it moves into the module's branch.
